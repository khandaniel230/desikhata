Weight and Mass Units

Weight is a measure of the force of gravity on an object, while mass is a measure of the amount of matter in an object. On Earth, weight and mass are often used interchangeably.

Conversion Formulas

  • Kilograms to Pounds: lb = kg X 2.20462
  • Pounds to Kilograms: kg = lb x 0.453592
  • Kilograms to Ounces: oz = kg X 35.274
  • Ounces to Kilograms: kg = oz x 0.0283495
  • Pounds to Ounces: oz = lb X 16
  • Ounces to Pounds: lb = oz X 0.0625

Reference Weights

  • Average human: 62 kg (137 lb)
  • Paperclip: 1 g (0.035 oz)
  • Basketball: 600 g (1.32 lb)
  • Elephant: 5000 kg (11,000 lb)

Historical Context

The kilogram is the base unit of mass in the International System of Units (SI). It was originally defined in 1795 as the mass of one liter of water. In 2019, it was redefined in terms of fundamental physical constants.
